Where the data comes from — and what was tested and rejected

Data: every merged pull request in PostHog/posthog over the 90 days , pulled from the GitHub GraphQL API one day at a time (so nothing is lost to the API's 1,000-result search cap), joined to the repo's git history for file-level data. Bot and automation accounts are excluded from every figure. Every score is a percentile within the cohort of ; click any number on the page for its formula and that engineer's inputs.

What stamphog actually is

An earlier draft of this dashboard read the stamphog label as "AI-authored". That was wrong, and it is corrected here. GitHub's own label description is "Request AI approval (no full review)" — the PRs are written by named human engineers; the label means the author asked a bot to approve rather than waiting on a full human review. These PRs are counted normally and simply attract little human review attention, which the Reviewed-throughput pillar already reflects without any special-casing.

Volume still leaks in

Bots author 71% of issue comments and over half of inline review comments in this repo, and AI reviewers comment roughly in proportion to diff size. An earlier version counted raw comment and thread totals, which quietly let lines-of-code back in through the pillar built to replace it. Attention now counts only human reviewers, human inline review comments and human issue comments, all excluding the PR author. That dropped the top-decile threshold from 6.5 to and this pillar's correlation with PR count from 0.98 to 0.86. The composite still correlates ρ=0.90 with merged-PR count, because percentiles of per-PR sums are partly rank transforms of PR count. It is reduced, not solved.

A change that was tested and rejected

To blunt the remaining volume sensitivity we tested adding median attention per PR as a density term. It was rejected on the evidence: it takes only 26 distinct values across 154 engineers (so one comment on one PR can swing a score more than the gap between 1st and 3rd), correlates just −0.28 with AI-approval share, and its largest beneficiaries are engineers with two to five PRs. It also penalises requesting AI approval on trivial changes, which is PostHog's own documented triage. Peak-based and reweighting alternatives were tested too and changed almost nothing. The honest conclusion was to fix the input rather than add a statistic, and to state the residual bias plainly on the page.

Research (SPACE, Forsgren et al. 2021; DORA; Deming) is consistent that individual ranking on delivery telemetry is unsound; those frameworks are deliberately scoped to teams and systems. Blast radius uses 90-day co-editing as a proxy for architectural centrality, not real dependency analysis.
